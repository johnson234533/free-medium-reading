Beginner Guide to Mediumship: How Spirit Communication Works

If you are new to the world of mediumship, understanding how spirit communication is said to work can feel both comforting and overwhelming at the same time. This beginner guide to mediumship and spirit communication is designed to give you a clear, compassionate foundation — explaining the key concepts, the process of a reading, and what you can realistically expect so that you can approach the experience with open eyes and an open heart. Whether you are grieving the loss of a parent, a partner, or a child, or whether you are simply curious about what mediums actually do, the information below is for you.

What Is Mediumship? Understanding the Core Concept

Mediumship is the practice of communicating with the consciousness — often called the spirit or soul — of a person who has physically died. A medium is an individual who claims to be sensitive to these non-physical presences and acts as an intermediary, or “go-between,” passing information and messages between the living and those who have passed on.

It is worth noting from the outset that mediumship is a spiritual practice, not a scientifically proven phenomenon. That said, millions of people around the world have found genuine comfort and healing through medium readings, and the practice has a long cross-cultural history spanning indigenous traditions, Spiritualism movements of the 19th century, and contemporary spiritual communities.

The Difference Between a Psychic and a Medium

People often use the terms “psychic” and “medium” interchangeably, but they mean different things in spiritual practice:

  • A psychic tunes into the energy field of a living person. They may pick up on emotions, life patterns, or potential future paths. Their focus is the living client.
  • A medium connects specifically with discarnate spirits — people who have passed away. Their focus is a third party who is no longer physically present.
  • A psychic medium does both: they can read a living person’s energy and also connect with spirits.

If your primary goal is to receive a message from a loved one who has died, you specifically want a medium or a psychic medium, not just a general psychic.

What Is Evidential Mediumship?

Evidential mediumship is widely considered the most rigorous and trustworthy form of spirit communication work. An evidential medium strives to provide verifiable details — specific names, dates, physical descriptions, personality traits, shared memories, or private circumstances that the medium could not have known through ordinary means. The “evidence” validates the identity of the spirit communicating, giving the person receiving the reading confidence that the connection is genuine rather than a lucky guess.

Examples of evidential details might include:

  • The spirit’s first name or nickname
  • A specific physical characteristic (a scar, a distinctive laugh, a way they always smelled of pipe smoke)
  • A private memory only the recipient would recognize
  • The manner of passing, described in accurate detail
  • A meaningful object the spirit had or gave to the recipient

When a medium provides this kind of specific, checkable information unprompted, it carries far more weight than general statements about love or guidance.

How Spirit Communication Is Said to Work

Understanding the mechanics — or at least the widely accepted spiritual model — of how communication happens can reduce anxiety and help you understand what to expect during a session.

The Idea of Energy and Consciousness

Most mediums work from a framework in which consciousness is not destroyed at physical death but instead transitions to a non-physical state. From this perspective, the personality, memories, and love of a person persist after the body ceases to function. Spirits are understood to exist at a different frequency or vibration than physical life, which is why an intermediary — the medium — is needed to bridge the gap.

The medium raises their own vibrational awareness (often through meditation, intention-setting, or prayer) while the spirit lowers theirs to meet somewhere in the middle. This mutual movement is sometimes described as “blending” — a merging of awareness between the medium and the communicating spirit.

The Three Clairs: How Information Arrives

Mediums receive information through what are often called the “clairs” — from the French word meaning “clear.” The four most commonly referenced are:

  1. Clairvoyance (“clear seeing”): The medium sees images, scenes, or symbolic pictures in their mind’s eye. They might describe a garden, a particular car, or a childhood bedroom.
  2. Clairaudience (“clear hearing”): The medium hears words, names, phrases, or tones internally — not with their physical ears, but within their awareness. A spirit might communicate their name, a familiar saying, or a piece of music.
  3. Clairsentience (“clear feeling”): The medium physically or emotionally feels what the spirit experienced — chest tightness associated with a heart condition, a sense of peace, or the spirit’s characteristic laughter.
  4. Claircognizance (“clear knowing”): The medium simply knows information without being able to explain how. A name, a date, or a fact arrives as certain knowledge without any sensory impression accompanying it.

Most working mediums use a combination of these channels, though many have a primary one that is strongest for them. A medium who works primarily clairsentiently might not give you vivid visual descriptions but might describe your grandmother’s warmth and humor with striking accuracy.

What Happens During the Spirit Communication Process

Here is a simplified version of what most evidential mediums describe when they talk about connecting with a spirit:

  1. Opening and attunement: The medium enters a receptive state, often through breathing, meditation, or prayer. They set an intention to connect for the highest good.
  2. Invitation: The medium opens themselves to whoever wishes to communicate — in many traditions, the medium does not summon a specific spirit but rather makes themselves available and sees who comes forward.
  3. Establishing identity: The spirit communicates identifying information — their relationship to the recipient, physical appearance in life, personality traits, or manner of passing.
  4. The message: Once identity is established, the spirit may offer messages of reassurance, guidance, resolution of unfinished emotional business, or simply the confirmation that love continues after death.
  5. Closing: The medium gently ends the connection, grounding themselves back into ordinary awareness.

Types of Medium Readings and What to Expect

One-to-One Private Readings

A private session between you and a medium is the most common and most intimate format. You will typically have 30 to 60 minutes of dedicated time. In a quality evidential reading, the medium should lead with evidence before delivering messages — meaning they should tell you specific details before asking you whether those details resonate.

What to expect in a private reading:

  • The medium may ask for your first name and possibly the first name of the person you hope to hear from, but a strong medium should need very little information from you
  • You will be asked to respond with yes, no, or I don’t know — not to elaborate or fill silence with information
  • You may feel emotional; this is completely normal and many people find readings cathartic
  • Not every detail will make immediate sense in the session — some validations surface hours or days later when you have had time to reflect or check with family members

Platform and Group Gallery Readings

In a gallery or platform mediumship setting, the medium works with a larger audience and brings through messages to multiple people. These are less personal than private readings but can still be deeply moving. If a message comes to you during a group event, you are under no obligation to respond if you are not ready.

Online and Phone Readings

Remote readings — conducted via phone or video call — are now widely available and many mediums report that physical distance makes no difference to the quality of the connection. Spirit communication is understood to operate beyond physical space, which is why an online reading with a compassionate, experienced medium can be just as meaningful as sitting in the same room. How to Prepare for Your First Medium Reading

Preparation matters — not because you need to do anything complicated, but because arriving with the right mindset makes for a much more open and meaningful experience.

Before Your Session

  • Give yourself quiet time beforehand. If you rush straight from a stressful meeting or a noisy environment, it is harder to be emotionally present.
  • Hold a photo of your loved one if you wish, but do not feel that you must. A medium does not need a photograph to connect.
  • Write down what you are hoping for. Are you seeking reassurance that your loved one is at peace? Do you have a specific question? Knowing your own intentions helps you evaluate the reading afterwards.
  • Keep an open mind about who comes through. You may be hoping for your mother but your grandfather steps forward first. Trust the process and allow the connection that wants to happen.
  • Do not coach the medium. Resist the urge to share details, confirm enthusiastically, or fill silences. The less the medium knows, the more meaningful the information they bring through independently becomes.

During the Session

  • Respond honestly with yes, no, or I don’t know. If you are not sure, say so.
  • If something does not make sense, say so calmly. A good medium will work with that, not pressure you to agree.
  • Take notes or ask if the session can be recorded. You may want to revisit details later.
  • If you feel overwhelmed, it is entirely acceptable to ask for a pause.

After the Session

  • Allow yourself time to process. Grief, relief, joy, and confusion often arrive together.
  • Reflect on any details that did not immediately make sense — speak with older family members who might hold pieces of context you were not aware of.
  • Honour your emotional response, whatever it is. Medium readings can stir feelings that have been suppressed since a loss.

Red Flags and How to Spot a Genuine Medium

This is important, and any honest resource on this topic must address it directly. Not everyone who presents as a medium is working with integrity. Here is what to watch for.

Warning Signs of an Unethical Practitioner

  • Fear-based messaging: Any practitioner who tells you there is a curse on you or your family that only they can remove — for a fee — is exploiting your grief. Genuine mediums do not use fear as a tool.
  • Fishing and cold-reading: If the medium asks many leading questions, makes only very general statements (“I sense someone older on your mother’s side”), and then waits for you to confirm before continuing, they may be reading your reactions rather than receiving spirit communication.
  • Excessive upselling: One reading should stand on its own. Pressure to book recurring sessions to “complete” a connection, or to purchase additional items to strengthen the link, is a red flag.
  • Guarantees: No genuine medium can guarantee they will connect with a specific person. Spirits are not on demand. Be cautious of anyone who promises this.
  • Vague generalities only: A reading comprised entirely of statements that could apply to anyone (“your loved one is at peace and wants you to be happy”) without any specific evidential detail is not evidential mediumship.

Signs of a Trustworthy, Ethical Medium

  • They lead with evidence before asking you for information
  • They are comfortable with you saying “I don’t know” or “that doesn’t fit”
  • They are transparent about how they work and their own limitations
  • They do not pressure you to book more sessions than you need
  • They hold appropriate boundaries and refer you to grief counselling or therapy if you seem to need additional support beyond what a reading can offer

Mediumship and Grief: What It Can and Cannot Do

Mediumship can be a meaningful part of a grief journey, but it is important to understand both its potential value and its limits.

How a Medium Reading May Help

  • Receiving evidence of continuity: Many people find that hearing specific, verifiable information — details a stranger could not have known — shifts their relationship to loss in a profound way. The grief does not disappear, but it is held differently when there is a sense that the bond continues.
  • Unfinished business: A reading may allow for the expression of things left unsaid — apologies, declarations of love, goodbyes that came too suddenly to be spoken.
  • Reassurance about the manner of passing: People bereaved by sudden death, accident, suicide, or illness often carry specific fears about whether their loved one suffered or whether they are at peace. A medium who can address these concerns evidentially can provide real relief.
  • A sense of ongoing connection: Signs and communication do not end when the reading does. Many people report that after a reading, they become more attuned to the small ways their loved one makes their presence known — a song on the radio at a meaningful moment, a butterfly, a smell with no obvious source.

What Mediumship Is Not a Substitute For

Medium readings are not therapy, counselling, or medical care. If you are experiencing complicated grief, depression, suicidal thoughts, or an inability to function in daily life, please seek support from a qualified mental health professional. A compassionate medium can be one thread in a broader tapestry of healing, but it is not the whole cloth.

Frequently Asked Questions Before Your First Medium Reading

How do I know a medium is genuine?

Look for evidential specificity. A genuine medium should bring through details — names, dates, physical descriptions, shared memories — that they could not have obtained from you or from easily researched information. Quality over quantity: five very specific, verifiable pieces of information are worth far more than twenty vague generalities.

Is it safe to contact someone who has passed?

From the perspective of most mediums and spiritual traditions, yes — reaching out with loving intent to someone you have lost is understood to be gentle and appropriate. You are not summoning or disturbing; you are opening a loving communication. If at any point a reading feels uncomfortable, you have every right to stop.

What if the person I want to contact does not come through?

This happens, and an honest medium will tell you so rather than manufacture a connection. Sometimes other spirits come through instead — a grandparent when you were hoping for a parent, for example. Some practitioners suggest that spirits communicate when they are ready, not necessarily on demand. It does not mean the loved one is not at peace or does not love you.

Do I need to believe in mediumship for it to work?

Most mediums say that a degree of openness is helpful, but deep belief is not required. Scepticism is fine; hostility is more of a barrier. Coming with a quiet, genuinely curious mind is usually enough.

How much does a medium reading cost?

Costs vary widely. Private sessions with experienced evidential mediums typically range from around £30–£80 in the UK or $40–$120 in the US for a 30–60 minute reading. Prices above this range are not uncommon for highly regarded practitioners. Be cautious of extremely low prices from unknown platforms, as quality can be inconsistent. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a psychic and a medium?

A psychic focuses on reading the energy of living people — their emotions, patterns, and potential futures. A medium specifically communicates with the spirits of those who have physically died. A psychic medium does both. If you want to connect with a deceased loved one, you need a medium or psychic medium, not a general psychic.

How can I tell if a medium is genuine or just cold-reading?

Look for evidential specificity. A genuine evidential medium leads with verifiable details — specific names, physical descriptions, shared memories, or facts about the manner of passing — before asking you questions. If a practitioner only makes vague, broadly applicable statements and waits for your reactions before continuing, they may be reading your responses rather than receiving spirit communication.

What should I do to prepare for my first medium reading?

Give yourself some quiet time before the session. Arrive knowing what you are hoping for, but stay open to whoever comes through. During the reading, respond with short yes/no answers rather than offering information. Take notes or ask if recording is permitted so you can revisit details later. Avoid coaching the medium — the less information you share upfront, the more meaningful the evidence they bring through independently.

What if the person I hoped to contact does not come through?

This does happen, and an honest medium will acknowledge it rather than invent a connection. Sometimes other spirits come forward instead. Most mediums work on the understanding that spirits communicate when they are ready, and a different spirit stepping forward does not mean your loved one is absent or at peace — it simply means the connection did not come through on that occasion.

Can a medium reading help with grief?

Many people find that receiving specific, verifiable evidence of a loved one’s continued existence shifts their experience of grief in a meaningful way. A reading may also allow for things left unsaid — apologies, expressions of love, goodbyes that came too suddenly. That said, mediumship is not a substitute for therapy or professional grief support. It can be one valuable element of healing, but anyone experiencing serious depression or complicated grief should also seek help from a qualified mental health professional.
