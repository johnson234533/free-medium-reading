Grief Support

You’re Not Alone in Your Grief

Losing someone you love is one of life’s most painful experiences. We’re here to support you — not just through readings, but through understanding.

Understanding Grief

Grief is not a linear process. There is no right way to grieve, and there is no timeline for healing. What matters is that you allow yourself to feel, to process, and to seek support when you need it.

How Medium Readings Support Healing

A medium reading is not a cure for grief — but for many people, it is a meaningful part of the healing journey. Connecting with a loved one in spirit can:

  • Provide reassurance that they are at peace
  • Deliver messages that bring comfort and closure
  • Reduce feelings of isolation and abandonment
  • Help process guilt, regret, or unfinished conversations
  • Remind you that love continues beyond physical life

When Is the Right Time for a Reading?

There is no perfect time. Some people seek a reading soon after their loss, while others wait months or years. The most important factor is that you feel emotionally ready and open to the experience.

If you are in acute grief — unable to function, unable to eat, or experiencing thoughts of self-harm — we gently recommend speaking with a grief counsellor or therapist first. A medium reading complements professional support but does not replace it.

Grief Resources

  • Crisis helplines — If you’re in crisis, please reach out to your local emergency services or crisis helpline.
  • Grief counselling — Professional grief counsellors provide structured support for your healing journey.
  • Support groups — Connecting with others who share your experience can be profoundly helpful.
  • Self-care — Be gentle with yourself. Grief takes time, and healing is not a straight line.

What Grief Can Look Like

Grief rarely arrives as one tidy emotion. It can show up as sadness, numbness, anger, relief, guilt or an odd calm, sometimes within the same hour. Physical effects are common as well: disrupted sleep, a changed appetite, difficulty concentrating, a heaviness in the chest. None of this means you are grieving incorrectly. It means you are responding to a significant loss.

Loss also comes in many forms. A parent, partner, child, friend or pet, a miscarriage, a long illness that ended slowly, or a relationship that was complicated: each carries its own weight, and each deserves to be taken seriously.

Ways to Support Yourself Between Sessions

  • Keep small routines. Regular meals, a short daily walk and a consistent bedtime give the days a frame when everything else feels unsteady.
  • Write it down. A letter to the person you lost, unsent and unedited, can hold what is hard to say aloud.
  • Make a small ritual. Lighting a candle on a certain day, visiting a favourite place or cooking a shared recipe gives your love somewhere to go.
  • Let people help in specific ways. Asking a friend to sit with you for an hour is easier than explaining everything you feel.
  • Pace the big decisions. Where possible, postpone irreversible choices such as moving house or clearing a whole home until the first waves have eased.

Supporting Someone Else Who Is Grieving

If you are reading on behalf of a friend or relative, a few principles tend to help. Listen more than you advise. Use the name of the person who died. Offer practical help such as meals or errands rather than the general “let me know if you need anything”. Remember that anniversaries and holidays can be harder than the first weeks, and a short message on those days is often remembered.

Signs It May Be Time for Extra Support

A medium reading is a complement to, not a substitute for, professional care. Consider speaking to a doctor, grief counsellor or therapist if you notice that:

  • you cannot manage basic daily tasks for an extended period;
  • you feel hopeless, or have thoughts of harming yourself;
  • you are using alcohol or other substances to cope;
  • the intensity of your grief is not easing at all over many months.

If you are in immediate danger or thinking about ending your life, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line in your country right away.

Going Into a Reading With Gentle Expectations

People often hope a session will deliver a single, conclusive message. In practice, comfort tends to arrive in small pieces: a detail that feels familiar, a phrase that sounds like someone you knew, a sense of being less alone. It is fine to cry, to pause or to end a session early. You set the pace.

What to Expect From a medium reading

A medium reading is a structured conversation in which a trained medium tunes in to the energy of your situation and shares what they perceive. The work isn’t about predicting fixed outcomes; it’s about giving you a perspective on the current shape of things, and the most likely trajectory if nothing significant changes. That perspective is often most useful for decisions, transitions, and patterns that have been hard to see from the inside.

Who a medium reading Tends to Help Most

  • People at a crossroads who want a clear outside view of the energetic landscape.
  • Anyone navigating a relationship dynamic that feels stuck or repetitive.
  • Clients dealing with a recurring pattern that hasn’t shifted despite their best efforts.
  • Those processing a transition – a move, a career shift, the start or end of a chapter.
  • Grieving clients who want gentle reassurance and connection.

How to Prepare for Your session

The 24 hours before your session matter more than people realise. Try to sleep enough, eat something grounding, and avoid stacking the day with stressful tasks. Set aside ten quiet minutes before the call to settle yourself, write down the questions actually circling in your mind, and let your nervous system soften. The medium can pick up far more clearly when your energy is settled rather than scattered.

Asking the Right Questions

The shift from prediction questions (“will X happen?”) to perception questions (“what’s the current energy of X?”) consistently produces richer, more useful readings. Bring a specific question, but hold it loosely. Some of the most striking moments in any session arrive when the medium surfaces something you didn’t know you needed to hear, in answer to a question you didn’t quite know you were asking.

What to Do After

Take fifteen quiet minutes after the session ends. Don’t immediately call a friend or scroll back into your phone. Let the session land. Write a few notes about what struck you most, what felt surprising, and any practical suggestions the medium offered. Return to those notes a few weeks later. The lasting value of a session is usually visible in the quiet review weeks afterwards, not in the moment of the call.

Why This Matters in 2026

The mediumship landscape continues to evolve, and what worked five years ago doesn’t always work now. Verified-purchase reviews are becoming standard. Cross-platform reputation tracking is replacing single-site reputation. Specialisation is overtaking generalist practice. The clients who do well in this market are the ones who keep learning how to read it – filtering carefully, sampling before committing, and developing trust with a small set of mediums rather than churning through new ones each time.

Practical Habits Worth Adopting

  • Filter by specialty rather than browsing top-rated overall. Specialty depth beats general popularity.
  • Read the longest recent reviews of any medium you’re considering. Length tends to indicate genuine engagement.
  • Sample with short sessions before committing to longer packages. Twenty minutes in two different sessions tells you more than an hour with one medium you didn’t pre-evaluate.
  • Cross-check ratings across at least one independent platform. Consistent ratings across multiple sites are far harder to manipulate than ratings on a single site.
  • Trust your post-session feeling more than aggregate ratings. After ten minutes with a real medium, you know far more than any review system can tell you.
