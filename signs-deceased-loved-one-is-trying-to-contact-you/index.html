10 Signs Your Deceased Loved One Is Trying to Contact You

Many people navigating grief quietly wonder whether the person they lost is somehow still present — and whether the small, unexplainable moments they notice are signs their deceased loved one is trying to contact you. These experiences are far more common than most people realise, yet many feel reluctant to speak about them for fear of sounding irrational. This guide takes those experiences seriously, explores what the research and experienced mediums say about them, and offers grounded, compassionate guidance for anyone seeking to understand what they may be receiving.

What Is After-Death Communication?

After-death communication (ADC) is a term used to describe a spontaneous or facilitated experience in which a living person perceives some form of contact from someone who has died. The phrase was coined by researchers Bill and Judy Guggenheim, who spent years collecting first-hand accounts from bereaved people across many cultures. Their findings, along with subsequent work by grief counsellors and spiritual researchers, suggest that these experiences are remarkably consistent across backgrounds, belief systems, and time periods.

It is worth being clear about what after-death communication is not. It is not the same as wishful thinking, though grief can certainly colour our perception. It is not always dramatic or impossible to miss. Most ADC experiences are quiet, brief, and easily dismissed — which is precisely why many people carry them in private for years without ever exploring them further.

Evidential mediumship, the kind offered by thoughtful practitioners, works differently from vague, generalised messages. An evidential medium aims to bring through specific, verifiable details — a nickname, a personality trait, a shared memory — that could not reasonably be guessed. This specificity is what separates meaningful mediumship from performance.

10 Signs Your Deceased Loved One Is Trying to Contact You

1. Vivid, Lucid Dreams That Feel Unlike Ordinary Dreams

Grief dreams are common and well-documented. Ordinary grief dreams tend to be fragmented, emotionally raw, and often distressing — the subconscious processing loss. But many bereaved people describe a second, very different category of dream: one where the deceased person appears calm, luminous, and fully themselves. These visits, often called visitation dreams, feel qualitatively different from ordinary dreaming.

Common features of a visitation dream include:

  • The loved one appears healthy and at peace, regardless of how they died
  • There is a sense of heightened vividness — colours more saturated, the environment more stable
  • The loved one communicates reassurance, often without moving their lips
  • On waking, the dreamer retains the experience with unusual clarity, sometimes for years
  • A feeling of having genuinely been together, rather than simply dreamed about someone

If you have had a dream like this, it is worth recording it in as much detail as possible as soon as you wake. Note what was said or conveyed, what your loved one looked like, and how you felt during and after. Over time, a pattern of such dreams may become one of your most treasured connections.

2. Unexplained Electrical Disturbances

Lights flickering, televisions switching on or off without reason, clocks stopping at a significant time, a phone screen activating without a touch — these are among the most frequently reported signs of spirit contact. Many mediums suggest that because consciousness operates as a form of energy, those who have passed may find it easier to interact with technology and electrical systems than to produce more complex physical phenomena.

The key distinction between a meaningful electrical event and a faulty appliance is context. A flickering light on the anniversary of a loved one’s passing, or when you are sitting alone thinking about them, carries different weight than a loose connection in an old lamp. When these events cluster around emotionally significant moments — a birthday, the moment you say their name aloud, a time they would have loved — many people find them impossible to dismiss.

3. Sensing a Presence in the Room

This experience is simple and common: you are in a room alone, and you have a clear, undeniable sense that someone else is there. Sometimes it is accompanied by a temperature shift — a warmth or coolness that seems localised to one area. Sometimes it happens in a place your loved one frequented during their life. Sometimes it comes without any physical correlate at all.

The sensation is typically described as unmistakably familiar — not frightening, but comforting. People often report that it feels exactly like the energy of the person they lost: the same quality of presence, the same emotional signature.

Many people dismiss this as their imagination because it leaves no tangible evidence. But across cultures and centuries, the perception of a deceased person’s presence has been reported too consistently and too similarly to be easily categorised as pure projection.

4. Finding Objects in Unexpected Places

Coins, feathers, butterflies, and certain flowers appear repeatedly in first-hand accounts as tokens left by those who have passed. What makes these experiences feel significant is not the objects themselves but the timing and specificity. A coin found in a spot that was thoroughly empty the day before. A butterfly that settles on your hand during a moment of deep grief. A particular flower — one your grandmother always grew — appearing in an unexpected location.

There is also a subtler version of this: objects associated with the deceased turning up in places they should not be. A photograph moving. A piece of jewellery appearing in a drawer where it was not placed. These small, specific dislocations often carry a sense of deliberateness.

5. Hearing Their Voice or Name

Some people report hearing the voice of a loved one — clearly, briefly, and usually at a moment of stress or sadness. This is distinct from the constant internal monologue of memory, where we reconstruct someone’s likely words. The reports describe something that seems to come from outside the self: a voice saying their name, offering a single reassurance, or asking them to pay attention.

Occasionally people describe hearing music their loved one favoured, played from no identifiable source, or a song that was significant to the relationship coming on the radio at a precisely resonant moment.

These auditory experiences are more common than is generally acknowledged, and they deserve to be taken seriously rather than immediately pathologised.

6. Signs in Nature — Animals, Birds, and Natural Phenomena

Cardinals, robins, butterflies, dragonflies, white feathers — specific animals and birds appear repeatedly in accounts of spirit contact, often carrying a sense of the loved one’s personality. A father who loved birds appearing as a robin that sits unusually close. A grandmother who loved butterflies, whose presence seems announced by one settling nearby at a moment of grief.

The animal is rarely the point in itself. What people describe is a quality of communication: the animal behaves unusually — staying longer than expected, making sustained eye contact, approaching without fear — and the observer has a felt sense of being addressed rather than simply observed.

Other natural signs include rainbows appearing at significant moments, sudden and localised fragrances (see below), or a shifting of the quality of light that feels meaningful rather than meteorological.

7. Smelling Their Fragrance with No Physical Source

This is one of the most universally reported and immediately recognisable signs. People describe catching the exact scent of a loved one’s perfume, pipe tobacco, baking, or some other strongly associated smell, in a location and at a time where no physical source exists.

The smell is typically brief — a few seconds — and often occurs when the person is thinking about their loved one or when a decision or difficulty arises that the loved one would have cared about. Many people describe an overwhelming sense of presence accompanying the fragrance, as though the scent is a calling card.

Because smell is one of the most memory-saturated senses, some will argue that these experiences are purely neurological reconstructions. But the specificity and timing, and the fact that they are often reported in locations the deceased never visited in life, gives many people reason to interpret them differently.

8. A Sudden, Inexplicable Feeling of Peace or Comfort

Grief is not a smooth or predictable experience. It tends to move in waves: moments of apparent calm followed by sudden surges of loss. Against that background, some people describe moments of profound, unexpected peace — a sense of being held or reassured that arrives without any deliberate effort to produce it.

These moments are often brief but unmistakable: a warmth that seems to come from outside, a lifting of weight, a clarity about something the person had been struggling with. They sometimes occur at the hardest moments — sitting with the reality of the loss, or facing a decision the loved one would have guided.

People who experience these moments often describe them not as projection but as reception: something arriving, rather than something constructed.

9. Synchronicities and Meaningful Coincidences

A synchronicity is a meaningful coincidence — an event that resonates with significance beyond what pure probability would suggest. In grief, synchronicities often involve numbers, dates, or symbols associated with the deceased appearing with unusual frequency or at precisely meaningful moments.

Examples people describe include:

  • Seeing the time 11:11 or another significant number repeatedly on the anniversary of a death
  • A specific piece of music associated with the loved one playing unexpectedly in multiple locations over a single day
  • A phrase the loved one often used appearing in an unrelated book, overheard conversation, and billboard within hours of each other
  • Recurring encounters with a symbol the loved one valued — a particular animal, a colour, a flower — clustering around emotionally significant moments

The interpretive step here matters. Synchronicities ask us to attend to meaning rather than causation. They do not prove contact, but they invite openness to the possibility.

10. Receiving Messages Through a Skilled Medium

Perhaps the most direct and structured way to explore whether your loved one is trying to reach you is through a sitting with an evidential medium. A skilled medium acts as an interpreter between the living and those who have passed, working to bring through specific, verifiable information rather than generalised statements.

What distinguishes a genuinely skilled medium from a vague, cold-reading performance is the quality and specificity of what comes through: a detail only you and your loved one would know, a personality trait rendered accurately, a memory tied to a particular moment or place. This specificity is the hallmark of evidential work.

How to Tell the Difference: Meaningful Contact vs. Wishful Thinking

This is an honest and important question to ask. Grief is one of the most powerful psychological states a human being can experience, and it does shape perception. So how do you hold these experiences with both openness and discernment?

Qualities That Tend to Distinguish Genuine Contact

  • Unexpectedness: The experience arrives without being sought or anticipated, often at a neutral or even distracted moment
  • Specificity: The detail — the fragrance, the word, the symbol — is precisely the person’s, not a generic grief response
  • Quality of feeling: People consistently describe a quality of peace, recognition, and calm that differs from emotionally driven imagination
  • Timing: Clusters of experiences around significant dates or decision points carry more weight than isolated incidents
  • Lasting impression: Genuine contact experiences tend to be retained with unusual clarity for months or years, unlike ordinary dreams or memories

How to Be More Receptive to Signs

Many people feel that once they stopped rushing past quiet moments, they began to notice what had always been there. Receptivity is not the same as credulity — it is a quality of attentive, grounded openness.

Practical Steps for Opening to Contact

  1. Keep a signs journal. Write down every experience, however small or uncertain, with the date, time, context, and your emotional state. Over weeks and months, patterns emerge that individual entries obscure.

  2. Create quiet time. After-death communication rarely breaks through noise and distraction. Moments of stillness — morning meditation, a walk in nature, sitting quietly before sleep — tend to be when contact is most often reported.

  3. Speak to your loved one directly. Many people find that addressing their loved one aloud, expressing what they need or miss, creates a kind of invitation. Whether or not this works in a literal metaphysical sense, it tends to open attention and awareness.

  4. Acknowledge what you notice. Rather than immediately explaining experiences away, allow yourself to sit with them. You can hold open the question — this might be contact — without requiring certainty.

  5. Consider a mediumship session. If you are hungry for more specific communication, an evidential medium provides a structured space. Look for someone whose approach is gentle, honest about what mediumship can and cannot do, and focused on specific evidence rather than general impressions.

  6. Connect with others who have had similar experiences. Grief support groups, online communities for bereaved people, and books of collected accounts (such as those compiled by researchers of after-death communication) can normalise what you are experiencing and offer frameworks for understanding it.

Working with a Medium: What to Expect

For many bereaved people, the desire for contact eventually leads them to consider a professional medium. If you have never done this before, it is natural to approach it with uncertainty — both hope and scepticism are reasonable.

Before the Session

  • You do not need to share details about your loved one in advance. A good evidential medium will work to bring through information they could not have known.
  • It is fine to come with questions in mind, but hold them loosely. What comes through may not be what you asked for — but may be exactly what you need.
  • Allow yourself to feel both hopeful and uncertain. Neither stance is wrong.

During the Session

  • Be honest in your responses. A genuine medium welcomes accurate feedback — “yes, that’s right” or “I don’t recognise that” — because it helps them navigate the connection. You are not helping by agreeing with things that don’t fit.
  • Specific, verifiable details are what matter most. Names, physical descriptions, personality traits, shared memories — these are the markers of genuine evidential work.
  • You may feel emotional. This is entirely normal and welcome in a thoughtful session.

After the Session

  • Allow time to sit with what came through. Some things that did not immediately register may become clear later, when you have had time to reflect or check with other family members.
  • Keep a record of the session, either as notes or a recording if the medium permits.
  • Trust your own sense of whether it felt genuine. A good experience brings peace and recognition; it should not leave you feeling manipulated or financially pressured.

Honouring What You Receive

However you interpret the signs you notice — as genuine spirit contact, as the mind’s loving tribute to someone treasured, or as something that simply resists easy categorisation — what matters most is that you honour the experience. Grief is not a problem to be solved. It is love with nowhere to go, and every sign that arrives — real or perceived — carries that love with it.

The experiences described in this article are among the most tender and human things a person can go through. They deserve to be treated with the same gentleness and respect that you would want extended to your grief itself.

If the signs you are noticing feel like more than coincidence, trust that curiosity. Explore it carefully, thoughtfully, and with as much openness as you can hold. And if you want a partner in that exploration, know that gentle, skilled support is available.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common signs a deceased loved one is trying to contact you?

The most commonly reported signs include vivid, peaceful visitation dreams, unexplained electrical disturbances, sensing a presence in a room, finding meaningful objects in unexpected places, catching their fragrance with no physical source, and receiving specific, verifiable messages through an evidential medium. These experiences tend to be brief, unexpected, and often cluster around emotionally significant dates or decisions.

How can I tell if a dream about a deceased loved one is a real visitation?

Visitation dreams tend to feel qualitatively different from ordinary grief dreams. The deceased person typically appears calm, healthy, and at peace; the dream has an unusual vividness and emotional stability; communication often happens without words; and the dreamer wakes with a strong sense of having genuinely been in the person’s presence. Ordinary grief dreams, by contrast, tend to be fragmented, emotionally distressing, and less clearly retained on waking.

Should I be concerned if I sense my deceased loved one’s presence?

In most cases, no. The experience of sensing a loved one’s presence after their death is very commonly reported and is typically described as comforting and familiar rather than frightening. It does not indicate a mental health problem. If the experience is distressing, or if you are struggling significantly with grief, speaking with a grief counsellor or therapist alongside any spiritual exploration is always a good idea.

What is an evidential medium, and how is it different from a general psychic reading?

An evidential medium focuses specifically on bringing through verifiable, specific information from someone who has passed — a name, a physical description, a personality trait, a particular memory — rather than providing general spiritual guidance or vague impressions. The emphasis on evidence is what distinguishes this kind of mediumship from broader psychic work. The goal is to provide information you can confirm, which supports trust in the connection.

How do I get started with a medium reading if I have never had one before?

Begin by finding a reputable, compassionate practitioner rather than making a rushed decision. Read about what to expect so you can approach the session with realistic openness. You do not need to share information in advance — let the medium work evidentially.
